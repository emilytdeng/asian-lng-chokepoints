## 1. Fleet constraint

Ship-time available to the Asian trade = least ship-time that could have delivered the 2025 baseline × (1 + 10% spare) = 5220 mtpa·days. The ships that carried the cargoes Asia can bid away from other buyers come with them (diverted mtpa × round trip to those buyers). "Ship-time needed" is the least needed to give every importer its equal-rationing share.

## 2. Shadow prices, Hormuz closed (equal-rationing LP)

Value of one more mtpa of capacity, in percentage points of λ (the share every importer receives). Constraints not listed have zero value.

| Points of λ per mtpa | Constraints |
|---|---|
| 0.318 | supply:OMN, divert:OMN, supply:USA, divert:USA, supply:RUS_ARC, divert:RUS_ARC, supply:AUS, divert:AUS, supply:BRN, divert:BRN, supply:IDN, divert:IDN, supply:MYS, divert:MYS, supply:PNG, divert:PNG, supply:RUS_SAK, divert:RUS_SAK, supply:OTH_ATL, divert:OTH_ATL, supply:OTH_PAC, divert:OTH_PAC, route:HORMUZ, pipeline:POS, pipeline:CAC, pipeline:MYC, pipeline:MYT, pipeline:POS2 |

Every binding constraint is worth the same: in a pooled market one more tonne from any non-Gulf exporter, any pipeline or a partly reopened Hormuz raises everyone's share equally. Regas, storage rates and every other route have zero shadow price.

Asia overall: observed −2% (Jan-Oct 2026); model equal-rationing −3%.

Reading: the physical model gets the size of Asia's loss about right once Hormuz is partly open. The assumed market order gets Pakistan right and Bangladesh too harsh, and gets India, Thailand and China wrong: China, with pipelines, storage and coal, stepped back and resold cargoes, while India and Thailand bought more.
