# Findings: Asian LNG chokepoint analysis

Generated by `npm run analysis` from data/network.json. Units: mtpa LNG-equivalent (1 Mt = 1.36 bcm). Diversion = 20% of non-Asian cargoes; crisis horizon 90 days; buffers used fuel-switch first, then storage.

Allocation views: **outbid** = the importer wins every cargo it can physically reach; **rationed** = every importer gets the same share λ of demand; **last in line** = the importer is served only after every other importer has taken all it can (loses every bidding war).

| 90 | 100% |
| 180 | 100% |

Storage counts at inventory ÷ closure length, so longer closures leave fuel switching as the only lasting buffer.
Without China's fuel switching and storage, λ with buffers (90 days) is 100%: China's buffers free LNG cargoes for everyone else in the pooled model.

## 5. Hypotheses (Hormuz closed)

**H1. South Asian importers are hit hardest and lose a bidding war.** Hormuz share of 2025 supply: Pakistan 99%, Bangladesh 64%, India 59%; vs Japan 6%, South Korea 15%, Taiwan 34%, China 17%, Thailand 16%. Last-in-line shares, worst first: India 0%, Taiwan 0%, Pakistan 0%, Bangladesh 0%, Thailand 23%, South Korea 24%, Japan 44%, China 70%. Outbid shares: JPN 100%, CHN 100%, KOR 100%, IND 100%, TWN 100%, THA 100%, PAK 100%, BGD 100%.

**H2. China's pipelines buffer it.** Pipelines cover 46% of China's import demand. China last in line: 70%; Japan 44%, Korea 24%, Taiwan 0%.

**H3. Taiwan's storage makes it the most fragile.** Days of cover under rationing: Taiwan 147, Japan 257, India 331, South Korea 460, Thailand 906, China ∞, Pakistan ∞, Bangladesh ∞. If last in line: Bangladesh 4, Pakistan 6, Thailand 10, Taiwan 11, India 14, South Korea 36, Japan 42, China 313.
